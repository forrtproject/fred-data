What the checks found

The frozen workbook contains 2,164 effect rows in 432 replication works. Source checks support corrections to 493 field instances across the seven audited fields, affecting 381 rows (17.6% of the workbook). This includes 31 coding omissions, where the matching primary source supplies a statistic absent from the coded field. Repeated original statistics across replication rows count as separate field instances.

There are 6,411 evaluable field instances: 5,918 match the source or documented calculation, and 493 require correction. The remaining 8,737 field instances are unverified or not comparable. Source access and target identification affect which fields can be checked, so the observed errors do not estimate a representative FReD error rate.

Complete passes, incomplete verification. All 2,164 effects have an audit checkpoint. A checkpoint recording an unavailable source, ambiguous target, or incompatible metric does not count as a verified field. The 80% complete-effect verification goal was not reached.

The deterministic screen was a separate diagnostic

The initial screen flagged 185 individual issues in 174 effects. After applying the confirmed patch in memory, 19 flags pass, 165 remain flagged, and one cannot be recomputed. Separately, 55 of the 174 flagged effects contain at least one confirmed error. Statistical inconsistency alone is not evidence that the FReD coding is wrong: a source may itself be inconsistent or require model information absent from the coded fields.

Method and decision rules

A fixed input and a separate audit

The audit froze the b48a7d6 FReD workbook and its codebook before checking sources. The workbook SHA256 is retained below. It contains seven target fields: original and replication analytic sample size, effect size or test statistic, p-value, and author-reported replication outcome. One-based audit row indices exclude the worksheet header; they are navigation aids, not durable merge keys. The audit retained every row and did not edit the shipped data, pipeline, main checkout, or FLoRA.

A deterministic screen to prioritise source checks

The screen checked positive integer sample sizes, p-value and correlation ranges, and whether recognised F, t, chi-square, z, or non-nested Pearson-correlation reports were compatible with coded p-values. It retained printed rounding intervals, p-value bounds, sample roles, nesting, and tail assumptions. Missing tails were marked as an assumed two-sided screen. No source-project rule was established to derive author-reported success automatically from significance or direction.

Blind primary-source extraction

Fresh Codex gpt-6.1-sol workers with medium reasoning received references, study identifiers, neutral descriptions, requested metric labels, and sanitised page/table or primary-source URLs. They did not receive shipped sample sizes, estimates, p-values, success labels, or the parent comparison output. Result-bearing descriptions were sanitised first; initial and later batches were inspected. Original and replication sides were extracted separately, with short literal evidence, printed page/table location, metric, bound, sample role, and exclusions.

Source retrieval was followed by separate title/author/DOI, version, pagination, and completeness checks. Scans and OCR required rendered target-page inspection. Published corrections took precedence where directly verified. An original statistic repeated only in a replication article or project summary remained unverified against the original primary source. Source acceptance did not itself establish a matching statistical target.

Comparison and independent adjudication

The parent checked source quotations and target identity before comparing values. Compatible rounding and explicit metric synonyms did not become errors. Metrics remained distinct unless a documented, reproducible transformation applied. Strict inequalities were preserved. Analytic N referred to the critical test after exclusions; participants, clusters, listings, observations, and study counts were not interchangeable.

Non-obvious disagreements went to independent Gemini 4 Argon (High) review with primary-source evidence and the unblinded coding context. Wrong-target blind extractions did not establish shipped errors. A test’s inferred degrees of freedom or N needed positive design, count, distribution, or p-value evidence; lack of an inline annotation was insufficient. Broad paper-level success labels were not assigned to unidentified component outcomes, and narrative success was not inferred from p-values alone.

Previously completed sides, verified fields, and settled decisions were protected during each source follow-up. Raw extraction and review outputs, exact evidence checks, unresolved scope decisions, and incremental counts are retained in the audit record. Original deadlines and completion markers stayed historical; separately authorised recovery passes did not restart them.

Interpretation and remaining limits

This is an AI-assisted, source-based coding audit, not an independent reproduction of every study’s raw-data analysis. Independent model review adds scrutiny but does not guarantee correctness. Coverage is selected by access, usable locators, compatible metrics, and identifiable outcomes. Unverified fields may be right or wrong; incompatible metrics are not automatically errors. A primary article’s own mistakes cannot be resolved solely by checking whether FReD copied it faithfully. Review the evidence and corrections before integrating them upstream.

Apply the confirmed changes in R

The package separates 493 audited field decisions from 920 physical cell updates: 491 value cells, 59 p-bound cells, 87 effect-source locators, 141 author-outcome quotations, and 142 quote-source cells. Two confirmed p-value errors change only the inequality. One existing outcome quotation already agrees after whitespace normalisation and is retained.

The patch does not change effect metrics, p-value tails, nesting, or unverified statistics. It does not remove records or convert unavailable reports into failed replications. Associated quote updates retain the exact short source text accepted in adjudication, including OCR spelling.

Download the correction package

Unzip the package beside the audited FReD.xlsx. Install readxl, writexl, and digest if needed, then run:

Rscript merge_fred_fixes.R FReD.xlsx FReD_corrected.xlsx

The command checks the original workbook hash and the two patch downloads, then writes a new data workbook and cell-level change log. Excel styling is not preserved. Existing output paths and the source workbook are never overwritten. To use an R object instead:

source("merge_fred_fixes.R")
fred <- readxl::read_xlsx("FReD.xlsx", trim_ws = FALSE)
result <- apply_fred_fixes(fred)
fred_corrected <- result$data
result$summary

Stop on a conflict. The merger checks entry_id + effect_id + fred_id, study/source/description/metric guards, and each expected old or already-patched value before making any change. Reordered rows work; reassigned identifiers or altered targets require a fresh evidence-based rebase. Do not force a row-number join onto a newer release.

The merger preserves row order, column order/types, and all cells outside the explicit patch. Reapplying it to the corrected R object is a no-op. It does not rebuild derived variables or rerun the preparation pipeline. Sixteen R integration checks passed, including reordered rows, conflicts without mutation, duplicate keys, altered downloads, safe reruns, output protection, all 493 field decisions, every p-bound, and an Excel round trip. The earlier 46 applicable audit checks and all 2,164 schema/export checks passed before this release.
