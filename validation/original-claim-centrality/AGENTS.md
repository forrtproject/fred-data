## Claim identity: clarified rule (user approved)

Identify the substantive claim represented by claim_text_o in the original article.
A numerical mismatch blocks centrality coding only when it makes that substantive
claim ambiguous. A uniquely identifiable relationship can be coded even when a
reported coefficient/sample cannot be matched to one table cell. Record the numerical
matching limitation separately; do not change statistics, signs, tests or estimands.
If prose and statistics point to incompatible substantive targets, explain that
ambiguity and leave centrality empty. Do not silently substitute another finding.

Independent Sol and Opus checking remains required for claims newly reconstructed
from statistics and for proposed claim-text corrections. This does not impose an
Opus-agreement gate on centrality coding of an otherwise clear textual claim.
Agents can record provisional statistical recoveries; the integrating agent must
keep genuinely unresolved recovered identities explicit pending independent checking.
Do not launch Opus from a Sol coding worker or apply a recovery overlay yourself.

## Claim-text replacements

Use verbatim source evidence linking the substantive and statistical claim. Quote length is not a target. Do not shorten clear existing claims. Propose changes only to fill missing claims or identify genuinely missing target/context, preserving relevant existing wording and qualifiers. Supporting excerpts for clear claims are evidence only.
Table excerpts keep the original labels; generic omission notices are unnecessary. Mark omissions inside prose quotations. A bare statistic,
table title or generated prose is insufficient. Store proposals in effect_uid-keyed
overlays with old/new text, original locator/hash and independent records. Keep clarification proposals separate until human review; never rewrite frozen source or raw coding. Preserve full raw outputs and earlier source snapshots in the private archive, not as duplicate public files.
Do not overwrite blinded labels after comparison. No numeric correction or changed
estimand is needed to make a centrality judgment; suspected data errors require
independent Opus verification before being presented as verified errors.
